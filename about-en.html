How to Play & FAQ

Apology Dojo is a quick, silly Japanese game. Tilt your phone forward to match a target bowing angle. Your current angle is never shown, so trust your feel, press OK, and see how close you were. The smaller the error, the more sincere your apology.

The basics

  1. Hold your phone upright with the screen facing you. Straight up is 0°; flat is 90°.
  2. Imagine the target angle and tilt the top of your phone toward you as if you were bowing.
  3. When you think you have the right angle, press the OK button.
  4. Your angle and the target are revealed. The smaller the error, the better the rating.

The modes

Official Apology (once a day)

Everyone gets the same 5 targets that day. The lower your total error over the 5 rounds, the higher you rank on the worldwide ranking. You can take the challenge once a day. The day changes at midnight Japan time, and you can also view yesterday's ranking.

The ranking is split into the “Sensor Division” (players who tilted their phones) and the “Slider Division” (players who used the on-screen slider). If you use the slider for even one round, that attempt counts as the Slider Division. You get one try per division per day, so after a Sensor Division attempt you can try once more in the Slider Division.

Ranked Match

A one-round, real-time duel vs a random player. You both get the same target, and the smaller error wins. Winning raises your rating and losing lowers it. Ratings start at 1000, and you earn ranks: Apprentice, Light Bow, Respectful Bow, Most Respectful Bow, Deep Apology and Dogeza. There is also a rating ranking.

Ratings and rankings are separate for the Sensor and Slider Divisions, and you are matched with players in the same division. If you cannot find an opponent, you can practice against the CPU (your rating does not change). Leaving mid-match counts as a loss, and if your opponent does not respond for 40 seconds, you win by forfeit.

Practice

A mode with no end. Targets come in 5° steps and the target shadow (in red) is visible, so it is a good way to get a feel for the angles.

Survival

You have 50 HP. Every degree of error drains it, and at 0 you are out. See how many stages you can clear. You are walking down a street, and the person you apologize to changes at every stage.

No-Miss

Miss by more than 10° and you are out. Each success raises your title. See how long a streak you can keep. You are walking down a street, and the person you apologize to changes at every stage.

Score Attack

Your score is the total error over 5 rounds. The lower, the better.

Play with friends

  1. One player presses “Create a Room” and gets a 4-character room code.
  2. Friends enter the code under “Join a Room” (you can also share an invite link).
  3. The host sets the number of rounds and starts, and everyone gets the same targets.
  4. The player with the lowest total error wins.

Works best on the same Wi-Fi. Mobile data may fail to connect.

Bowing angle guide

In Japanese business etiquette, a light bow (eshaku) is about 15°, a respectful bow (keirei) about 30°, and the most respectful bow (saikeirei) about 45°. For apologies, the most respectful bow (around 45°) is used. In this game, targets are named by angle: up to 20° is a Light Bow, up to 35° a Respectful Bow, up to 55° the Most Respectful Bow, up to 75° a Deep Apology, and anything beyond that is a Dogeza (kneeling bow).

FAQ

The angle does not change when I tilt, or it says the sensor is unavailable

The game uses your phone's motion and orientation sensor. The first time you play, your phone may ask whether to allow sensor access; please allow it. If it did not ask, or you refused by accident, reload the page or reset this site's permission in your browser's site settings. On devices without a sensor, you can set your angle with the on-screen slider.

Can I play on a computer?

Yes, but computers have no tilt sensor, so you will use the slider. Playing on a phone is recommended.

The angles feel slightly off

Sensor values can differ a little between phones and how you hold them. In Settings, press “Set Current Angle as 0°” to make your current holding angle 0° (press “Reset” to go back to straight upright).

Can I change the name shown on the ranking?

Edit the “Name” field on the menu, and your next attempt will be registered under the new name. Records already registered cannot be changed. Please do not use your real name or anything that identifies you. Abusive, discriminatory or sexual words, names impersonating the operator, and contact details such as email addresses or phone numbers are not allowed (they are rejected when typed, and shown as “＊＊＊” on other players' screens).

It is once a day, but I was able to play again

“Once a day” is tracked per browser. If you clear your browser data or open the game in a different browser, you are treated as a different player. Thank you for your understanding.

I cannot enter the Sensor Division in Ranked Match

The Sensor Division is only for devices whose tilt sensor works. On devices without a working sensor (such as computers), you can play the Slider Division. On a phone, choosing the Slider Division also lets you play with the on-screen slider.

What happens to my rating if I clear my browser data?

Your rating is tied to an anonymous ID stored in your browser, with no account registration. If you clear your browser data or play in a different browser, you start again from 1000 as a different player. Thank you for your understanding.

What if my connection drops during a match?

If your opponent does not respond for 40 seconds, the remaining player wins by forfeit. If you closed the game mid-match, it is recorded as a loss the next time you open Ranked Match.
